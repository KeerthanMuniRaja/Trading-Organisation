## Owner's intent

The organisation should develop new specialists rather than indefinitely repeat the work of its first bots. A new bot learns in school and college, graduates, and contributes alongside existing teams. It may learn from experienced bots while bringing a distinct capability or assignment.

Outstanding R&D may justify a new department and team. Periodic hackathons create opportunities for new candidates and improvements to existing bots. As demonstrated revenue and resources grow, the organisation should increase useful capability and collaboration.

Bots that cannot justify an active role should transfer what can be learned from their experience and leave active service. The organisation's knowledge should survive the retirement of individual bots.

## Opportunity discovery

Day-to-day news, magazines, articles, business and sales developments, company affairs, market data, and other relevant internet sources feed the research process.

R&D looks for unmet questions, weak coverage, changing conditions, new hypotheses, or workloads the existing team cannot serve adequately. An apparent gap is recorded with evidence; it is not immediate permission to create more bots.

Examples of differentiated candidates:

| Existing capability | New proposal | How the contribution differs |
|---|---|---|
| General company-news analysis | Supply-chain disruption specialist | Connects upstream events to exposed companies and tests transmission timing. |
| Earnings-event analysis | Guidance-versus-realised-performance specialist | Evaluates a different evidence set and forecasting horizon. |
| One market monitor at full capacity | Partitioned monitors for separate markets | Adds measured coverage while avoiding overlapping task ownership. |
| One forecasting method | Independently evaluated alternative method | Must show useful error diversity or incremental predictive value. |

These are illustrative assignments, not selected investment strategies.

## Lifecycle

1. **Propose:** record the opportunity, existing coverage, intended novelty, mentor, costs, and success criteria.
2. **Admit:** check overlap, permitted scope, resources, data access, and fitness evaluation.
3. **School:** learn common skills, source integrity, tool use, market mechanics, uncertainty, and constitution.
4. **College:** specialise through curated material, verified predecessor experience, experiments, and exercises.
5. **Graduate:** pass an independent examination and demonstrate a contribution beyond the current team.
6. **Join:** receive a bounded role and explicit work ownership, initially under probation where appropriate.
7. **Develop:** accumulate traceable evidence, cooperate, and propose qualified improvements.
8. **Review:** promote, retain, narrow scope, retrain, move to reserve, or retire based on role-specific evidence.
9. **Transfer and retire:** preserve useful experience, reconcile unfinished work, revoke active permissions, and remove the runtime when the exit criteria are satisfied.

The [bot proposal](templates/bot-and-department-proposal.md), [graduation review](templates/graduation-review.md), and [retirement record](templates/knowledge-transfer-and-retirement.md) make these transitions reviewable.

## No redundant work

Reusing a model, code library, verified lesson, or established skill is allowed. A separate bot identity must still justify its work.

Distinguish four cases:

- **Capability expansion:** a different task, method, horizon, sector, or evidence perspective.
- **Capacity expansion:** the same qualified capability serves distinct work partitions because demand exceeds capacity.
- **Validation replication:** an explicitly labelled independent check with a bounded purpose.
- **Unjustified duplication:** another bot repeats the same assignment without adding useful coverage, reliability, or independent evidence.

The first three can be useful. The fourth should fail admission or trigger consolidation.

Maintain a capability register and task-ownership records. Assess similarity of outputs and errors, shared data/model dependence, and incremental team performance. Different prompts or names alone do not establish independent thinking.

## Hackathons

Hackathons are periodic, bounded research events. Their cadence and resource budget remain open; no event has been scheduled.

Proposed process:

1. Select an evidence-backed challenge and a measurable baseline.
2. Publish eligible data, permitted tools, resource limits, scoring rules, and protected evaluation boundaries.
3. Allow teams and new candidates to propose differentiated hypotheses.
4. Record inherited ideas, novel contributions, all experiments, costs, and failures.
5. Evaluate entries independently on quality, robustness, usefulness, and resource efficiency.
6. Identify promising candidates for further education, challenger evaluation, or department proposals.
7. Store verified lessons and useful negative results from every entry.
8. Route any model-training or deployment change through ordinary qualification.

A hackathon winner earns further consideration, not unrestricted deployment. Hidden test results should not become training material while still serving as an examination set.

Use the [hackathon record](templates/hackathon-record.md).

## Fitness and selection

Fitness should compare a bot with a role-specific baseline over sufficient evidence and relevant conditions. Candidate dimensions include task quality, calibrated uncertainty where applicable, net economic contribution, avoided failures, reliability, collaboration, incremental information, and resource use.

Trading performance should account for costs and exposure. Research, risk, source verification, and recovery can justify their place without directly booking revenue. Useful dissent should be recognised.

Do not eliminate a specialist solely because of a short losing period, age, temporary inactivity, or low agreement with the majority. Some capabilities matter mainly during uncommon conditions. Reserve status and later requalification preserve that option.

A weak bot's failure can still yield useful lessons. Preserve the failure evidence without presenting its unsupported conclusions as knowledge.

## Revenue-linked expansion

Revenue and validated opportunity should support sustainable growth, not unchecked reproduction.

A proposed expansion review should establish:

- A continuing opportunity or measured capacity shortage.
- Benefit beyond the present team and realistic alternatives.
- A resource envelope that the organisation can sustain.
- Added compute, data, maintenance, coordination, and supervision costs.
- Operational readiness and a qualified lead.
- Stop, rollback, and review conditions if the expected value does not appear.

The agreed [wallet policy](12-wallet-and-treasury.md) retains 60% of newly eligible realised net profit in Wallet 1 and protects 40% in Wallet 2. Expansion can use only available, authorised Wallet 1 funds under approved department budgets; it cannot retrieve Wallet 2 money or trigger SBI top-ups. Reserves, exact cost treatment, and budget amounts remain open. Paper gains, gross turnover, and one unusually profitable result must not be treated as automatically available expansion funds.

Before a spawning policy is authorised, expansion remains a proposal. Once authorised, routine creation can occur within its limits, with lineage, quotas, and owner visibility.

## Creating a department

A department needs a distinct ongoing mandate that is better served by an organised team than by a single existing bot. Record its interfaces, qualified roles, recurring workload, evidence of value, resource needs, and overlap with current departments.

Outstanding performance can support that case. Department creation follows the owner-defined authority policy; it does not let a winning bot write its own governance rules.

## Knowledge transfer before deletion

Proposed exit procedure:

1. Stop new assignments and identify unfinished work.
2. Transfer or resolve active tasks and any external side effects.
3. Capture skills, evidence, experiment history, decision records, negative results, and failure conditions.
4. Independently label material as verified, disputed, obsolete, sensitive, or untested.
5. Index useful material with its source, authoring version, context, and applicability.
6. Test representative retrieval or reproduction by a successor.
7. Preserve required audit records and any approved recovery snapshot under the retention policy.
8. Revoke credentials and permissions, terminate the active runtime, and remove it from scheduling.
9. Notify the owner of the reason, transferred knowledge, and residual concerns.

"No knowledge goes to waste" is a preservation objective, not a guarantee that every raw item is correct, lawful to retain, or useful forever. Do not copy secrets into shared memory. Bad conclusions belong in labelled failure records, not active teaching material.

Active deletion must not erase accountability. The precise retention and deletion policy remains an open decision.
