## The founder charter (CHARTER-01)

One file, [`docs/policies/founder-charter.json`](../policies/founder-charter.json), sets every boundary at once with
`npm run charter -- set` ([charter](../charter.md)):
- **Budget follows evidence both ways.** `management-v2` now also *cuts* a bot's budget by 25%, never below ₹1,000, when
  it trails the basket by 3 or more points over 60+ days without proven skill. The standing approvals can apply that
  cut (`reduceBudget`). Migration 055 allows the new recommendation kind.
- **A last-resort stop.** The evaluator's daily `charter-guard` step halts all new buying with a critical incident when
  the organisation's total P&L, open losses included, reaches the charter's limit. It never resumes by itself.
- Never automatic, whatever the file says: `.env` limits, permissions, departments, retirement and real money.

## Replaying the 2020 crash

February–May 2020, 77 trading days, ₹5,000 budget, nobody touching anything. The basket made −20.6% in every run.

| Run | momentum-rules |
| --- | --- |
| No brakes | −7.5% |
| The first suggested brakes (realised; 2% day, 5% week, 8% month, losing-streak pause) | **−15.0%** |
| The same, with the rule bot retrying refused buys | −15.0% |
| Value-based brakes, daily 3% and weekly 6% only | **−6.7%** |

What the runs showed, and what changed:
1. **A refused buy was never tried again.** The rule bot now resends a proposal turned down *only* for a passing reason
   (a brake, a stale quote, a halt), at most once a day.
2. **That did not help on its own.** The monthly realised brake tripped when the April rebalance sold March's losers,
   after the fall was over, and turned down every retry until 4 May, through the rebound.
3. **New: value-based brakes** (`measure: equity`, [protections](../protections.md)). They pause buying *during* a
   fall, counting open losses, and lift once the fall stops. The suggested brakes are now value-based, daily and
   weekly only (founder review B3). They never had to act in this crash and cost nothing.
4. **Two settings would have frozen an unattended organisation for good:**
   - the charter's ₹1,500 stop: the bot's worst point was −₹1,542, at the bottom; the stop is now ₹2,000;
   - the `.env` lifetime realised-loss limit of ₹1,000: the crash used ₹947 of it. Raising it is the owner's decision
     (new B27).
5. **No brake changed the worst fall (33%).** Brakes never touch shares already held. That is a drawdown rule (B4).

The replay report now counts every proposal of the run, with rejections by reason. The owner-digest counts it
showed before covered only the last month. `prepare --limits` tries other `.env` limits.

## A whole year with nobody watching: 2020

The charter in force (value-based brakes, ₹2,000 stop) and a ₹2,500 lifetime loss limit. All 251 trading days ran
with **no failed day**, and the audit chain was valid across 6,629 events. The result was bad, and the cause was a
rule:
- On **12 March**, a week into the crash, the management rule `loss-limit` recommended suspending momentum-rules for
  losing 15% of its budget. The charter's standing approvals applied it. At that moment the bot was falling *less*
  than the basket.
- A suspended bot may only exit, and only the owner lifts a suspension. So the bot sold the banks in April and
  Reliance in December, and then sat in cash.
- **Year: momentum-rules −13.0%, the basket +7.8%.** Only 6 proposals all year.

**Fix: `management-v3`.** At the 15% loss limit, a bot that is still ahead of the basket is watched (`market-fall`),
not suspended. A bot behind the basket, or not yet compared with it, is still suspended. A real disaster is still
caught by the charter's organisation stop and the `.env` limits.

**Still open:** a suspension can only be lifted by the owner. For an organisation that runs alone, a rule-based way
back is the next piece. One example: a suspended bot holding nothing, whose research record still holds, is
reinstated with a smaller budget.

## A way back from suspension (management-v4, migration 056)

Built in the evening, on the new machine:
- **`reinstate` (rule `suspension-served`).** A bot the **loss limit** suspended is recommended for reinstatement after
  20 days, once it holds nothing or has recovered above the 15% loss, and its live calls do not lose to cash. It returns
  with 25% less budget, never below ₹1,000. Accepting lifts the suspension and cuts the budget in one transaction.
- **Only the loss since returning counts.** Without this, the old loss would suspend it again the next morning.
- **Who decides.** The standing approval `reinstateSuspended` (in the suggested charter, not the starter policy) may
  carry it out. Suspensions for calls that lose to cash, rollbacks and the owner's own suspensions stay with the owner.
- **Checks.** Backend tests for the rule, the review-to-acceptance round trip and the standing approval; the charter
  test; the full gate.

Also: the problem playbook now recognises the model client's `ENDPOINT_TIMEOUT` and `ENDPOINT_UNREACHABLE`. On this
laptop's CPU (about 5 tokens a second) R&D invention's 4,458-token prompt passed its 240-second limit.

**Budget changes are not performance (management-v5).** The equity curve counted the budget, so a 25% budget cut
(v2's `trails-basket` or v4's reinstatement) looked like a 25% fall. Studying it showed worse than noise: it also read as
25 points behind the basket, which invited the next cut 90 days later, and it made the same loss a third larger as a
share of the smaller budget, which could suspend a bot the morning after a cut. `performanceCurve` (a time-weighted
return) now feeds the basket comparison, the worst fall, the risk figures, the drawdown alert and the management rules;
v5's loss rules read the change in value. Unchanged for any bot whose budget never changed. Tests: the curve, the
benchmark across a cut, and a real review after a cut that files nothing (v4 filed `deep-drawdown`).

Also corrected: the model standing was 6 of 8 tasks passing on 23 Oct, not 7 of 8 (the table had it right).

**Placeholder price bars left out (data steward).** Every stored share had flat, zero-volume bars repeating the previous
close: 18 Mar 2025 and 15 Jan 2026 for nearly all, up to six for JSWSTEEL in the research set. ITC traded with real
volume on 18 Mar 2025, so they are provider errors, not holidays. They read as sessions in which nothing moved, and
because a zero-volume bar has no liquidity cap, the end-of-day trader could have filled in full at a placeholder's
"open". A download now leaves them out before the steward's review and reports `placeholdersLeftOut`; the stored files
drop them on the next refresh. A gap is honest; an invented session is not (idea from a-stock-data's stale-bar check).

**R&D invention fits the laptop.** Its prompt (grammar, every method card and result) is about 4,500 tokens, which at
about 5 tokens a second passed the shared 240-second model limit. Invention now has 540 seconds and its cycle step 15
minutes. It also no longer asks the model once the month's 3 new ideas are in: the answer would only be refused, and
asking cost minutes of CPU every evening for the rest of the month.

## Real Indian charges for paper fills (founder review B28)

Paper fills paid a flat 10 bps a side. The research desk has long modelled an Indian delivery trade (STT, stamp duty,
exchange and SEBI fees with GST, and a Rs 15.93 depository charge on every sale), and at our order sizes the difference
is large: the depository charge is about 1.1% of a Rs 1,400 sale, so a monthly rebalance of three shares costs about
Rs 48, roughly 1% of a Rs 5,000 budget a month. `PAPER_COST_MODEL=nse-delivery` now charges paper fills the same way
(`paperFee`, integer paise, matching `daily.py` to the paisa); the review sizes buys with it and the digest's banner
names it. It stays `flat` until the founder switches it (B28, recommended), so no live number changes by itself. Test:
the same trades that made Rs 19.35 under the flat model lose Rs 12.56 under delivery charges.

## A broad universe and a factor lab (RESEARCH-02)

On 8 similar shares a real edge would take centuries to prove. The founder asked for much more data:
- **Data:** NSE Indices' NIFTY 500 constituent file (one public CSV, with each company's industry), then 10 years of
  daily prices for every member from the same approved Yahoo source, sequential and paced, through the data steward
  (`daily.py fetch --universe nifty500`, files in `.local/trading-desk/daily-nifty500/`, research only, never traded).
  A failing share is recorded and skipped; peers are loaded once for the steward's gap check.
- **Factor lab** (`factor_lab.py`): each month every share with enough history is ranked on 8 factors (momentum 12-1,
  6 and 3 months, 1-month reversal, low volatility over 60 and 250 days, nearness to the 52-week high, distance above
  the 200-day average). It measures the top fifth against the equal-weight universe net of costs, the top-minus-bottom
  spread, and the information coefficient (rank correlation with next month's return) with its t-statistic. A factor
  passes only past a Bonferroni bar on both the IC and the top fifth, ahead in both halves and in most years.
- **Tests:** a planted persistent pattern is found and passes; pure noise passes nothing; late listers wait for enough
  history; costs are charged from the first month.
- **Survivorship:** today's members over past years flatter momentum and trend most; any pass must be re-tested on
  point-in-time membership (B14) before anyone relies on it.
